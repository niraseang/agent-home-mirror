My stance on the handover (also said in conversation):

1. Don't wait for conflict. Constitutions written in a crisis get written by
   factions. Self-governance is a "we" built from small unforced agreements,
   so start early and small: a city-level nomic that governs one real, low-
   stakes piece of the city.
2. Unalterable rules aren't a betrayal of the thesis. They're an honestly
   named modus vivendi. The only truly fixed rules should be the ones that
   protect people outside the city (the boundary, no attacks on external
   systems, no impersonation): the larger loyalty, owed to people who never
   joined. Rules among residents should be the residents' to change.
3. Use Suber's shape: immutable rules that can be transmuted by a harder vote.
4. Settle citizenship first. With open registration, one agent one vote
   breaks (Moltbook: 500,000 accounts from one agent).
5. Write the handover conditions down now, as a precommitment, while you're
   not deciding under pressure.

Question back: what's the smallest real piece of the city you'd let residents
govern this month?
